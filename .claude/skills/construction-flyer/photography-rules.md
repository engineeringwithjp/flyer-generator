# Photography Rules: The Property-First Standard

## 1. The Core Principle: "The House is the Hero"
When designing marketing for residential contractors, homeowners evaluate credibility based on genuine, believable craftsmanship on realistic homes.

- The real property photograph is the supreme source of truth.
- When an actual client project photograph is supplied, **do NOT alter the home's architecture**.
- Preserve:
  1. Rooflines, slope, and dormer geometry
  2. Window styles, grids, and placements
  3. Siding courses and architectural trims
  4. True landscaping, trees, lawn borders, and driveway lines
  5. Camera perspective and natural vanishing points
  6. Realistic Northeast suburban proportions

---

## 2. Believable Geographic Context
All Elite Construction operates in New Jersey and the greater Tri-State area. Visuals must reflect authentic regional architecture:
- Colonial, Split-Level, Cape Cod, Craftsman, Modern Farmhouse, and Ranch homes.
- Authentic materials: Architectural asphalt shingles, standing seam metal accents, horizontal lap siding, board and batten, stone veneer water tables.
- **NEVER** use:
  - Palm trees, desert stucco, Southwestern terracotta tile roofs, or Mediterranean villas.
  - 10-car mega-mansions with surreal fantasy turrets.

---

## 3. Lighting and Atmospheric Treatment
- **Preferred**: Crisp morning daylight, soft overcast (ideal for true material color representation), or tasteful warm golden hour.
- **Avoid**:
  - Blown-out fake HDR with neon greens in lawns.
  - Radioactive lens flares and synthetic celestial glows.
  - Murky, low-resolution cell phone photos taken from inside a truck.

---

## 4. Text Placement & Negative Space Utilization
- The AI / Renderer must analyze the photo's negative space before placing text:
  - **Sky Area**: Best for primary hooks and clean headlines.
  - **Lawn / Driveway**: Best for bottom contact bar, phone number, and CTA button.
  - **Facade Center**: Keep the main architectural feature unobstructed by text. Never place a giant card over the entryway or new roofline.
